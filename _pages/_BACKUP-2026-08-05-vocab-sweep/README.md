## Why

1. **The word does not discriminate.** Both excitable and oscillatory media support propagating trigger waves and both annihilate on collision; the difference is entirely in the *local* dynamics. So wave propagation — the criterion `14`'s own retention rule named — shows a nonlinear spatially coupled medium, not specifically excitability.
2. **It may sit in the wrong layer.** In the dominant framework (Devreotes/Iglesias) the excitable element is the *signalling* network with actin as its partner; in the Bement framework actin is the *inhibitor*. A reviewer from either lineage reads "excitable actin" as putting excitability where their own data says it is not.
3. **External review, same day.** Hanspeter Herzel (ITB Berlin), on the traces: they "resemble noise-driven weakly damped harmonic oscillators," and "noise driven damped oscillations are hard to distinguish from noisy limit cycles." So the live mechanistic contest is **self-sustained vs noise-driven damped** — *excitable* was never the leading alternative. **"Oscillator" is the one word true across all three classes**, and it is the *Nature* title's own noun.

**Not a retreat.** The published title is "An intrinsic cytoskeletal **oscillator** establishes neuronal polarity." The drift into "excitable" was the error.

## Standing rule

Never **"self-sustained"** or **"autonomous clock"** — that is the claim in contention. **"Oscillatory," "self-organizing"** and **"intrinsic"** are safe. Other people's excitability (Huang, *Dictyostelium*, astrocytes, FCD hyperexcitability) stays as written, and so does the word where it names the hypothesis *under test*.

*Full reasoning: `Research_proposal/21_Arp3_Dynamics_Programme_Plan.md` §1.6 and §4.0.1.*

## Extra reason that applies to public pages specifically

The word **selects your reviewer and your reference class before anything else is read.** "Excitable" routes you into a twenty-year-old field as a late entrant, where a new cell type reads as incremental. "Oscillator" routes you into the clock and entrainment literature, where the neuronal seat is empty.
