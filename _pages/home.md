<section class="editorial-section" aria-labelledby="next-question">
<div class="section-heading"><p class="eyebrow">The independent program</p><h2 id="next-question">From axon selection<br>to persistent identity.</h2></div>
<div class="section-body">
<p class="section-lead">My first-author <em>Nature</em> study established how recurrent cytoskeletal dynamics contribute to axon selection. My independent program asks what happens next: how the selected identity persists through continued remodeling.</p>
<div class="question-row"><span class="question-number">01</span><div><h3>When does an axon become difficult to reassign?</h3><p>Follow the same neuron through a transient perturbation and recovery to distinguish renewed growth from retained or reassigned axonal identity.</p></div></div>
<div class="question-row"><span class="question-number">02</span><div><h3>When can another neurite retain axonal identity?</h3><p>Promote one competing neurite locally, withdraw the perturbation, and ask whether an additional axon persists.</p></div></div>
<p class="tissue-note"><strong>The tissue context.</strong> Compare matched cortical neurons in culture and developing tissue to ask where the environment changes acquisition or persistence.</p>

<div class="investigator-copy"><p class="eyebrow">About Tien-Chen Lin</p><h2 id="investigator-title">Molecular mechanism.<br>Whole-cell behavior.</h2>
<p>I am a cell biologist and biochemist trained in Taiwan and Germany. My work connects the assembly of cytoskeletal machinery with the behavior of developing neurons.</p>
<p>I led the experimental development and quantitative analysis of the neuronal oscillator study, built specialist collaborations, and drafted the manuscript. My earlier work combined protein purification and biochemical reconstruction to explain microtubule nucleation.</p>
<p>That combination shapes my approach: locate a causal difference in a living cell, then identify and test the molecular mechanism that explains it.</p>
