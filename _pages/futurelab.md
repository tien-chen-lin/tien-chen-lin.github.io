I am applying for group-leader positions and am beginning to build a research group at the interface of **cell biology, neuroscience, and biophysics**. The group will study **how neuron–glia interfaces control cytoskeletal oscillators**, and how a transient cytoskeletal decision is consolidated into persistent neural architecture. We work across the full causal chain: biochemical reconstitution, acute optogenetic control in living cells, quantitative live imaging, sparse perturbation in tissue, and dynamical modeling.

### Who I am looking for

Once a position is secured, I will be recruiting:

- **PhD and rotation / M.Sc. students** eager to combine imaging, molecular tools, and quantitative analysis.
- **Postdocs** with a background in cytoskeletal cell biology, neuroscience, biophysics, protein biochemistry, or computational biology.

### What the group will offer

Interdisciplinary training that blends reductionist biochemistry with holistic *in-vivo* approaches, a collaborative and supportive environment, and projects with both mechanistic depth and translational relevance.

### Working with me

A few principles I hold to, so you know what to expect:

- **Project ownership.** You will own a project, with guardrails scaled to your stage — my aim is that you leave able to frame a question, build the tools, and defend the answer.
- **Frequent, concrete feedback**, in both directions. Small and often beats rare verdicts, and I want your feedback on the project and on my mentoring.
- **Quantitative training that travels.** Coding, image analysis, and modeling are part of the job here, whatever you arrive knowing.
- **Reproducibility as a default.** Analysis code and methods are written to be re-run by someone else — including by you, a year later.
- **Career advocacy.** I have navigated this job market myself, and I will be candid about trade-offs and spend my capital opening doors for you.

### Collaborate

I bring acute optogenetic control of cytoskeletal proteins, quantitative live-cell state inference, and *in-vitro* reconstitution of nucleation machinery — and I am always glad to apply them to a partner's system. I am actively looking to connect with groups working on glial biology, brain tissue mechanics, and dynamical modeling of cellular systems.

If any of this sounds like you, I would be glad to hear from you — please reach out by [email](mailto:tien-chen.lin@dzne.de) with a short note about your interests.
