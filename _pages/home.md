I am a cell biologist and biochemist working on **cytoskeletal state control** in neurons. My research showed that neuronal polarity is not dictated part by part but *selected* — by an intrinsic ARP2/3–actomyosin oscillator running across the whole cell (*Nature*, 2026). The independent program now moves from the neuron alone to its physiological interface with glia, and to the problem of **consolidation**: how a transient cytoskeletal decision becomes persistent neural architecture.

### Three questions

- **What are the states?** Which variables distinguish oscillatory exploration, persistent growth, retraction, and stable polarization — and can a model of them predict an unseen perturbation?
- **What controls them?** Which class of glial input actually moves the neuronal system between states, and by what input–output law?
- **What makes a state persist?** How actin–microtubule organization consolidates a selected state — and what makes that consolidation fail.
